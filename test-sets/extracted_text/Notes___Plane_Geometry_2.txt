Different units of angles 
1. Degree – The degree is a non-SI unit 
approved in 1969 by the General 
Conference on Weights and 
Measures. This unit was established 
by the Babylonians more than 4000 
years ago. A degree is divided into 
60 minutes; a minute is divided into 
60 seconds. 
 
2. Radian – The radian is the standard 
angular measure in the International 
System of Units (SI). A radian is the 
angle between two radii of a circle 
which cut off on the circumference 
an arc equal in length to the radius. 
 
3. Gon – The gon or centesimal degree 
is 1/400th of the full circle. A right 
angle is 100 gons. A gon is divided 
into 100 centesimal minutes; one 
centesimal minutes is divided into 
100 centesimal seconds. This unit is 
also known as grad or grade and is 
used in surveying works and aircraft 
navigation. 
 
4. Mil – The mil is a measure of an 
angle which is 1/6400 of the full 
circle. A right angle is 1600 mils. 
Other Types of Angles 
 
1. Adjacent angles – two angles with a 
common leg 
 
 
2. Complementary angles – two angles 
whose sum is a right angle or 90 or 
/2 radians 
 
 
Angle A and angle B are complementary 
angles. Angle A is the complement of angle 
B and vice versa. 
 
3. Supplementary angles – two angles 
whose sum is a straight angle or 180 
or  radians 
 
Angle A and angle B are supplementary 
angles. Angle A is the supplement of angle 
B and vice versa. 
 
4. Explementary angles – two angles 
whose sum is a perigon or 360 or 2 
radians 
 
 
Angle A and angle B are Explementary 
angles. Angle A is the explement of angle B 
and vice versa. 
 
5. Vertical angles – angles formed by two 
intersecting lines. Vertical angles are 
equal. 
 
Angle A and angle B are vertical angles. 
These angles are called vertical angles 
because each side of one is an extension 
through the vertex of a side of the other. 
 
 
For easy conversion from one unit to 
another, just remember the measure 
of an angle with corresponding value 
in one revolution. Then apply ratio and 
proportion!

Polygon 
A plane figure with three or      
more angles. It has as many     
sides as angles. The sides of the 
polygon are straight lines. The 
term “polygon” comes from Greek 
words “poly” which means      
“many” and “gonia” which      
means “angles”.
