If two elements in a permutation of distinct elements 
are in reverse order relative to their normal or 
natural order, they constitute an inversion. A 
permutation is said to be even if it contains an even 
number of inversions; it is odd if the number of 
inversions is odd.  
 
The number of transpositions that are required to 
return a sequence of elements to their natural order 
is even or odd according to the number of inversions 
in the arrangement. 
 
Example: 
 
In the permutation (C, E, D, B, A) 
 
Element C precedes B and A, therefore has 2 
inversions. 
 
Element E precedes D, B and A, therefore has 3 
inversions. 
 
Element D precedes B and A, therefore has 2 
inversions 
 
Element B precedes A, therefore has 1 inversion. 
 
The total inversion is 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 8. So 
permutation (C, E, D, B, A) is an even permutation.
